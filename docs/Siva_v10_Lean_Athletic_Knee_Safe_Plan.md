# 1. GOALS AND SUCCESS MARKERS

## Main outcomes

- Train every major muscle at least **twice per week**.
- Build a **lean athletic V-shape** through the upper chest, lats, upper back and side/rear shoulders, with proportional arms, a smaller waist, athletic glutes and balanced legs—not maximum size for its own sake.
- Train the complete core: anti-extension, anti-rotation, lateral stability and controlled trunk flexion.
- Improve knee strength, tracking, balance and confidence without repeatedly provoking swelling or instability.
- Reduce abdominal fat gradually while preserving muscle, energy and hair health.
- Improve cardiovascular fitness without depending on cycling while it remains irritating.
- Keep the program sustainable within one morning session.

## How progress will be judged

Track all four—not only body weight:

1. Waist measurement
2. Front, side and back photographs
3. Strength and repetition progress
4. Knee pain, swelling, warmth and stability

A light six-pack requires both developed abdominal muscles and sufficiently low abdominal fat. More crunches cannot selectively remove stomach fat.

## Lean-athletic physique priorities

The program intentionally gives the highest visual and functional priority to:

1. **Upper chest, lats and upper back** for a stronger V-taper
2. **Side and rear shoulders** for shoulder width without chasing oversized traps
3. **Glutes, hamstrings and knee-tolerated quads** for athletic lower-body proportions
4. **Carries, core stability and conditioning** for posture, movement quality and work capacity
5. **Moderate direct arm volume** because presses, rows and pulldowns already train the arms

Small-muscle work for the neck, hands, feet, face and pelvic floor remains low-dose functional training. It should not steal recovery from the main athletic muscle groups. Most working sets stop with 2–3 good repetitions available rather than using failure training.


## What “every muscle” means in this plan

The body has hundreds of skeletal muscles, and it is neither practical nor useful to isolate every individual muscle. This plan instead covers:

- Every major movement-producing skeletal muscle group
- Small stabilizers that matter for posture, joint health and athletic function
- Neck, forearm, hand, lower-leg and intrinsic-foot muscles
- Facial-expression muscles as optional gentle mobility rather than heavy resistance work
- Male pelvic-floor and penile-root support muscles relevant to continence and sexual function

The **body of the penis is mainly erectile tissue, blood vessels, connective tissue and smooth muscle—not a skeletal muscle that can be enlarged through ordinary strength training**. The trainable skeletal muscles associated with the penile root and pelvic floor include the **bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus and levator-ani group**. These are addressed through controlled pelvic-floor contractions and, equally importantly, full relaxation.

The eye muscles, tongue, swallowing muscles, intestinal smooth muscle, heart muscle, erectile-tissue smooth muscle and other involuntary muscles are not appropriate bodybuilding targets. Normal daily function, breathing and cardiovascular training already use many of them.

### Bulgarian split-squat rules

Keep the exercise because it usually improves your knee strength and balance, but make it conditional rather than compulsory.

#### Green Day — perform it

- Starting pain 0–2/10
- No warmth, thickening or swelling
- No current instability or giving-way sensation
- Use one-hand support
- Use a low rear-foot support
- Use body weight or light dumbbells
- Use a comfortable depth rather than chasing maximum depth
- Lower under control for approximately 3 seconds
- Keep the front knee tracking toward the second or third toe
- Stop with approximately 3 repetitions still possible

#### Yellow Day — use a reduced unilateral or short-arc option

When the knee feels mildly stiff, tender or uncertain, do **not** automatically repeat Tuesday's sit-to-stand. Choose the first option that remains smooth and pain-free:

1. **Supported Static Split Squat, rear foot on the floor** — 2 x 6–8/side, body weight, shallow range and one-hand support; or
2. **Progressive Short-Arc Quad Extension or Terminal Knee Extension** — 2 x 15–20/side with slow control.

If neither is comfortable, use the high-bench sit-to-stand only as a fallback. Reduce depth or load by 25–50% and do not progress that week.

#### Red Day — no loaded knee-bending exercise

When there is increased warmth, swelling, giving-way, limping or pain of 5/10 or higher, replace it with:

- Quad Set — 2–3 x 10 contractions
- Straight-Leg Raise — 2 x 10–12, if pain-free
- Short-Arc Quad Extension — 1–2 x 12–15, if pain-free

Do not combine Bulgarian split squats with step-ups, deep sumo squats and wall sits in the same workout. Start once weekly. Consider a second weekly exposure only after at least four consistently symptom-free weeks.

# 7. MALE PELVIC-FLOOR AND SEXUAL-FUNCTION RULES

Pelvic-floor contractions recruit a group rather than one perfectly isolated muscle. The relevant trainable muscles around the penile root and pelvic outlet include the bulbospongiosus, ischiocavernosus and levator-ani group.

## Weekly pattern

- **Contraction-focused days:** Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday
- **Relaxation-focused days:** Wednesday and Friday
- Use moderate effort, not repeated maximal squeezing.
- Completely relax between repetitions and keep breathing.
- Do not tighten the glutes, thighs or abdomen to fake the contraction.
- Do not practice by repeatedly stopping urine flow. That may be used once to identify the muscles, not as the exercise itself.
- Reduce volume and seek pelvic-floor guidance if contractions cause pelvic pain, urinary urgency, constipation, pain with ejaculation or difficulty relaxing.

Pelvic-floor training may support continence and sexual function, but erection quality also depends heavily on cardiovascular health, sleep, metabolic health, stress and medical factors. Kegels are supportive work, not a guaranteed penis-enlargement method.

# 8. KNEE-SAFETY SYSTEM

## Green

- Pain 0–2/10
- No increased warmth or swelling
- No instability
- Knee returns to its normal baseline by the next morning

**Action:** Continue and progress gradually.

## Yellow

- Pain reaches 3–4/10
- Mild thickening, tenderness or stiffness appears
- Symptoms settle within 24 hours

**Action:** Reduce range, weight or repetitions by 25–50%. Replace the provoking movement and do not progress that week.

## Red

- Increasing warmth or visible swelling
- Giving-way, locking or limping
- Pain 5/10 or higher
- Symptoms last beyond 24–48 hours or worsen

**Action:** Stop the provoking exercise. Use rest, wrapped ice for up to 15–20 minutes, light compression and elevation. Arrange clinical assessment.

## Next-morning rule

An exercise is truly tolerated only when the knee is at its normal baseline the following morning. Temporary relief after massage is not enough evidence to continue loading it.

## Cycling return

Cycling is removed for now because it has repeatedly provoked symptoms. Reintroduce it only after the knee has remained consistently calm and preferably after a physiotherapy assessment:

1. Start with 5 minutes at very low resistance.
2. Keep the seat high enough to avoid deep knee bending.
3. Add 1–2 minutes before adding resistance.
4. Stop if warmth, swelling, instability or next-day symptoms appear.
5. Intervals and high resistance come last.

# 10. INTENSITY, REST AND PROGRESSION

## RPE and repetitions in reserve

Most working sets should finish at approximately **RPE 7–8**, meaning about 2–3 clean repetitions remain possible. Muscle fatigue is acceptable; joint pain, warmth or instability is not.

## Rest periods

- Rest approximately 60–75 seconds after completing both exercises in an upper-body superset.
- Rest approximately 90–120 seconds after demanding lower-body supersets.
- Rest 30–45 seconds between isolation, balance and rehabilitation movements.

## Weeks 1–2: technique and knee baseline

- Use 2 working sets for most exercises.
- Keep 3 sets only for the first main pair.
- Finish with approximately 3 repetitions still possible.
- Record the knee response during training and the following morning.

## Weeks 3–6: progressive training

- Use the listed sets.
- Finish most sets with approximately 2 repetitions still possible.
- Maintain controlled technique and the knee traffic-light rules.

## Double-progression rule

Increase weight only when:

1. You reach the top of the repetition range on every set with clean form;
2. Approximately 2 repetitions remain possible; and
3. The knee has no adverse response by the next morning.

Then add the smallest available load increase, approximately 2.5–5%, and return to the lower end of the repetition range. For planks, carries, balance and rehabilitation exercises, add repetitions or time before adding load.

## Deload

Use a deload during **week 7**, or sooner if fatigue, joint irritation, poor sleep or declining performance accumulates:

- Reduce sets by approximately 30–50%.
- Use lighter loads.
- Keep yoga, gentle Kegels and symptom-free rehabilitation.
- Do not chase personal records.

| Water | **2.5–3.0 litres**, plus more with sweating or Arizona heat |
| Fruit | **2 servings** |
| Vegetables | **At least 3 cups** |
| Added sugar | Preferably **25–30 g or less** |

A practical centre target is:

> **140 g protein + 220 g carbohydrate + 65 g fat = approximately 2,025 kcal.**

Carbohydrate supports morning training, recovery and performance. The primary calorie leaks to control are sugary shakes, fried snacks, desserts, alcohol and unplanned grazing.

# 12. EXACT PER-MEAL TARGETS

## Recommended option — three planned meals

This is the easiest structure for reaching the protein target without making meals excessively large.

| Meal | Calories | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat | Fibre |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Meal 1 — after morning workout** | ~620 kcal | **45 g** | **70 g** | **18 g** | **~10 g** |
| **Meal 2** | ~720 kcal | **50 g** | **80 g** | **22 g** | **~12 g** |
| **Meal 3** | ~685 kcal | **45 g** | **70 g** | **25 g** | **10–13 g** |
| **Daily total** | **~2,025 kcal** | **140 g** | **220 g** | **65 g** | **32–35 g** |

## Alternative — two main meals plus a post-workout mini-meal

| Eating occasion | Calories | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat | Fibre |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Post-workout mini-meal** | ~245 kcal | **25 g** | **25 g** | **5 g** | **~5 g** |
| **Main meal 1** | ~825 kcal | **55 g** | **95 g** | **25 g** | **~13 g** |
| **Main meal 2** | ~955 kcal | **60 g** | **100 g** | **35 g** | **14–17 g** |
| **Daily total** | **~2,025 kcal** | **140 g** | **220 g** | **65 g** | **32–35 g** |

A strict two-meal schedule would require approximately 70 g protein, 110 g carbohydrate, 32–33 g fat and 16–18 g fibre in each meal. It can work, but the portions may be uncomfortable and harder to sustain.

## Per-meal quality rule

Each meal should contain:

- Its complete protein target
- A high-fibre carbohydrate source
- Vegetables and/or fruit
- A measured healthy-fat portion
- Water rather than a sugary drink

A protein shake may close a genuine protein gap, but it counts toward daily calories. A sugary milkshake is dessert, not a free recovery drink.

# 13. FOOD-QUALITY FRAMEWORK

No fixed menu is required. Build meals using these categories.

## Protein

Use eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, paneer in controlled portions, tofu, soy, dal, beans, lean meat or whey protein as needed.

## Carbohydrates

Use rice, oats, potatoes, whole-grain bread or chapati, legumes and fruit. Adjust portions rather than eliminating carbohydrates.

## Fat

Use measured portions of nuts, seeds, olive oil, avocado, egg yolks and other minimally processed fats. Oils, fried food and nuts can raise calories quickly even when the food is considered healthy.

## Fibre and micronutrients

Include vegetables, legumes, fruit, whole grains and nuts/seeds regularly. Include a calcium-rich food or fortified alternative daily.

## Flexibility rule

- Aim for **80–90% minimally processed food**.
- Keep 10–20% flexible for planned treats.
- Plan fried food or dessert instead of grazing unpredictably.
- Avoid casually drinking calories.
- Do not begin high-dose iron, zinc, biotin or other hair supplements without a demonstrated need.

# 14. HAIR, TEETH, SLEEP AND APPEARANCE SUPPORT

## Hair

- Avoid crash dieting or rapid weight loss.
- Maintain protein consistently.
- Include dietary sources of iron, zinc, vitamin B12, vitamin D and omega-3 fats.
- Seek testing rather than blindly supplementing if shedding increases.
- Manage stress and sleep consistently.

## Teeth

- Brush for two minutes twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between the teeth once daily.
- Reduce the frequency of sugary and acidic drinks and snacks.
- Rinse with water after shakes, coffee or acidic foods.
- Avoid sipping sugary drinks over several hours.
- Maintain dental checks according to the dentist's recommendation.

## Overall appearance and recovery

- Target **7.5–8.5 hours of sleep**.
- Use sunscreen on exposed skin.
- Maintain hydration and regular grooming.
- Keep posture work in the program: rows, face pulls, rear-deltoid work and carries.

## Every two weeks

- Waist measurement at the same location and time
- Front, side and back photographs under similar lighting
- Energy and hunger review
- Main-lift top sets: bench/press, row or pulldown, RDL and Bulgarian split squat or replacement

## Calorie adjustments

- If average weight and waist do not decrease for **3 consecutive weeks**, reduce calories by approximately 100–150 kcal.
- If weight falls faster than approximately 0.5 kg per week, strength declines, energy is poor or hair shedding increases, add approximately 100–150 kcal.
- Do not reduce protein when adjusting calories.

# FINAL DIRECTION

Follow the plan for the first two weeks without chasing heavy weights. The immediate objective is to identify movements that the knee tolerates consistently, establish the meal targets and learn the added small-muscle exercises without fatigue or strain. The physique target is lean, broad-shouldered and athletic—not maximum bulk. Progress comes from months of repeatable training, controlled nutrition, conditioning and recovery—not from seven days of punishment squeezed into one heroic week.
