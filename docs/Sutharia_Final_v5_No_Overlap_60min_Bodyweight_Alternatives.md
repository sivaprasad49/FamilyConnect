# 3. BODY-WEIGHT ALTERNATIVE SYSTEM

## When gym equipment is unavailable

Use the alternative shown in the final column of each table. The body-weight version may not perfectly reproduce a cable or heavy dumbbell movement, especially for vertical pulling and heavy grip work, but it preserves the movement pattern and keeps the session productive.

## How to make body-weight work challenging

Use one or more of these methods:

1. Slow the lowering phase to **3–4 seconds**.
2. Pause for **1–2 seconds** in the hardest safe position.
3. Progress from wall → bench → floor angles.
4. Use one side at a time when balance and joints allow.
5. Increase repetitions within the listed range.
6. Add a controlled isometric hold after the final repetition.
7. Use self-resistance from the opposite hand.
8. Stop most working sets with approximately **2 good repetitions still possible**.

## Safety

- Do not use unstable doors, folding chairs, glass tables or lightweight furniture for support.
- Use a solid wall, stable bench, floor or sturdy chair.
- No pull-ups, bar hangs or hanging knee raises are required.
- Equipment availability should change the variation—not cancel the workout.

# 13. JOINT-INFLAMMATION TRAFFIC-LIGHT SYSTEM

## Green day

- Pain 0–2/10
- No increased warmth or swelling
- Normal movement and stability
- Back to baseline the following morning

**Action:** Complete the plan and progress gradually.

## Yellow day

- Pain 3–4/10
- Mild stiffness, tenderness or inflammation
- Symptoms settle within 24 hours

**Action:**

- Reduce weight, range, repetitions or sets by 25–50%.
- Use the body-weight alternative.
- Keep cardio easy.
- Do not progress that week.

## Red day

- Visible swelling, redness or increased warmth
- Major loss of function
- Pain 5/10 or higher
- Symptoms persist beyond 24–48 hours or worsen

**Action:** Stop loading the affected joint. Use comfortable mobility or rest and obtain medical guidance for significant or recurrent symptoms.

# 14. MENSTRUAL READINESS GUIDANCE

Use actual symptoms rather than rigid calendar-day rules.

## Train normally when

- Energy and sleep are reasonable.
- Bleeding and cramps are manageable.
- Joints feel stable.
- The warm-up improves movement.

## Reduce training by 20–40% when

- Cramps, heavy bleeding, migraine or fatigue are significant.
- Joint inflammation increases.
- Sleep is poor.
- The warm-up does not improve discomfort.

Options:

- Drop one set from each exercise.
- Use RPE 5–6 rather than 7–8.
- Choose the easier body-weight variation.
- Replace strength training with yoga and walking when symptoms are substantial.
- Avoid forceful breathing practices when uncomfortable.

# 15. PROGRESSION

## Weeks 1–2 — technique and baseline

- Use two working sets for most exercises.
- Keep three sets only for the first main pair.
- Finish with approximately three repetitions still possible.
- Record joint comfort during the session and the next morning.

## Weeks 3–8 — progressive training

- Use the listed sets.
- Finish most sets with approximately two repetitions still possible.
- Increase repetitions before increasing weight.

## Double progression

For an exercise listed as **3 × 8–12**:

1. Reach 12 clean repetitions on every set.
2. Keep approximately two repetitions in reserve.
3. Confirm that joints feel normal the next morning.
4. Add the smallest available load.
5. Return to approximately 8–9 repetitions.

For body-weight exercise:

1. Reach the top of the repetition range.
2. Slow the movement.
3. Add a pause.
4. Progress the angle.
5. Use a more challenging unilateral variation only when control is good.

## Deload

Every seventh or eighth week—or earlier when fatigue accumulates:

- Reduce working sets by 30–50%.
- Use lighter loads or easier body-weight variations.
- Keep comfortable yoga, cardio and mobility.
- Do not chase personal records.

# 18. SLEEP, HAIR, TEETH AND RECOVERY

## Sleep

Aim for:

> **7.5–8.5 hours of sleep each night**

- Treat approximately 7 hours as the routine minimum rather than the ideal target.
- Aim closer to 8–9 hours when recovery is poor, training stress is high or sleep debt has accumulated.
- Keep wake time reasonably consistent.
- Reduce bright-screen exposure and intense work close to bedtime.
- Do not use hard exercise to compensate for chronic sleep loss.
- Persistent loud snoring, gasping or excessive daytime sleepiness deserves medical discussion.

## Hair

- Avoid crash dieting and rapid weight loss.
- Maintain protein consistently.
- Include food sources of iron, zinc, vitamin B12, vitamin D and omega-3 fats.
- Seek testing rather than starting high-dose supplements blindly if shedding increases.

## Teeth

- Brush for two minutes twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between the teeth once daily.
- Reduce the frequency of sugary and acidic drinks and snacks.
- Rinse with water after coffee, shakes or acidic foods.
- Follow the dentist’s recommended check-up schedule.

## General recovery and appearance

- Maintain hydration.
- Use sunscreen on exposed skin.
- Break up prolonged sitting with brief movement.
- Retain rows, rear-deltoid work, wall slides and carries for posture.
- Do not turn Monday or Thursday recovery sessions into hidden high-intensity workouts.

# 19. TRACKING

## Workout log

Record:

- Date
- Exercise or body-weight variation
- Weight used
- Repetitions completed
- RPE or repetitions in reserve
- Joint response
- Next-morning response

## Weekly

- Body weight on 3–7 mornings; use the weekly average
- Completed workouts
- Average sleep
- Joint symptoms
- Menstrual readiness
- Average daily protein consistency

## Every two weeks

- Waist and hip measurements
- Hunger and energy review
- Main-exercise progression

## Every four weeks

- Front, side and back photographs under similar lighting
- Cardio tolerance review
- Exercise confidence and joint-comfort review

## Calorie adjustment

- When average weight and waist do not change for three consecutive weeks, reduce calories by approximately 100–150 kcal.
- When weight falls faster than approximately 0.4–0.5 kg per week, strength declines, energy is poor or hair shedding increases, add approximately 100–150 kcal.
- Do not reduce protein when adjusting calories.

# FINAL DIRECTION

Follow the plan for at least eight weeks before making major structural changes. During the first two weeks, learn both the equipment version and the no-equipment alternative for every key movement.

The goal is not to fill 60 minutes with unnecessary repetitions. The goal is a repeatable weekly rhythm with adequate recovery, progressive strength, complete muscular coverage, controlled nutrition, cardio fitness and healthy joints.

The desired result is petite, strong, lean-curvy and athletic: rounded glutes, balanced legs, a firmer waist, defined posture muscles, lightly toned arms and shoulders, good energy and sustainable long-term fitness.
