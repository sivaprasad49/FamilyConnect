# SIVA — LEAN ATHLETIC, KNEE-SAFE TRAINING & NUTRITION PLAN

**Profile:** 37-year-old male | 164 cm | approximately 72 kg  
**Primary goal:** Build a lean, athletic V-shaped physique with broader shoulders and upper back, visible upper chest and lats, proportional arms, a tighter waist and light abdominal definition, athletic glutes and legs, complete functional muscle coverage, and a stronger, more stable right knee—without training for maximum bodybuilding size.

**Available training time**

- **Monday–Friday:** maximum **50 minutes**
- **Saturday and Sunday:** approximately **70–90 minutes**
- **Saturday and Sunday are both training days**
- One complete session may be performed in the morning or evening

**Relevant condition:** Previous right kneecap dislocation with occasional pain, warmth/thickening, swelling or instability. Cycling, stairs, incline walking and some knee-dominant movements can occasionally aggravate it. Supported Bulgarian split squats are retained only on symptom-free Green days because they have personally felt strengthening and stabilizing.

> **Medical safety note:** This plan is a training framework, not a diagnosis. Recurrent warmth, swelling, locking or giving-way after a previous kneecap dislocation deserves assessment by a sports physiotherapist or orthopaedic clinician. Seek prompt care if the knee becomes clearly hot/red, rapidly swollen, locked, repeatedly gives way or cannot bear weight.

# 1. PLAN DESIGN

## Weekly structure without major-muscle overlap

| Day | Session | Time | Training load |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Monday** | Active recovery + knee rehabilitation + soleus + neck/hands/feet | 45–50 min | Recovery |
| **Tuesday** | Upper Body A + core | 45–50 min | Strength |
| **Wednesday** | Lower Body A + knee control | 45–50 min | Strength |
| **Thursday** | Upper Body B + core + rotator cuff | 45–50 min | Strength |
| **Friday** | Mobility + safe conditioning + small-muscle maintenance | 40–50 min | Recovery |
| **Saturday** | Lower Body B + complete lower-leg work + core | 70–90 min | Strength |
| **Sunday** | Athletic V-taper upper body + carries + core + conditioning | 70–90 min | Strength |

## Why this sequence works

- Sunday’s upper-body session is followed by Monday recovery rather than another upper-body workout.
- Upper-body strength is distributed across Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday.
- Lower-body strength is distributed across Wednesday and Saturday.
- Tuesday–Thursday alternates upper → lower → upper, so the same major muscles are not trained hard on consecutive days.
- Friday prepares the joints and nervous system for the longer weekend sessions.
- Saturday is lower-body dominant and Sunday is upper-body dominant.
- Five strength days and two active-recovery days are retained.

## What “all muscles” means

The body contains hundreds of skeletal muscles. It is neither practical nor useful to isolate every individual muscle. This plan covers:

- Every major movement-producing skeletal-muscle group
- Major posture and joint-stabilizing muscles
- Neck, forearm, hand, lower-leg and intrinsic-foot muscles
- Complete trunk function: flexion, anti-extension, anti-rotation and lateral stability
- Male pelvic-floor support muscles through low-dose contraction and relaxation practice
- Optional gentle facial-expression mobility

The heart, intestinal muscles, erectile-tissue smooth muscle, eye muscles and other involuntary muscles are not bodybuilding targets.

# 2. EQUIPMENT AND BODY-WEIGHT ALTERNATIVE RULES

## Available equipment when accessible

- Dumbbells
- Adjustable bench
- Functional trainer or cable station
- Resistance bands
- Flat treadmill
- Stable chair, wall and floor space

## When equipment is unavailable or occupied

Use the body-weight alternative shown beside each exercise. A body-weight alternative does not always reproduce the exact loading of a cable, dumbbell or machine, especially for vertical pulling and heavy grip work. It preserves the movement pattern and training continuity until equipment becomes available.

## Make body-weight exercises challenging safely

Use one or more of these methods:

1. Slow the lowering phase to **3–4 seconds**.
2. Pause for **1–2 seconds** in the hardest safe position.
3. Use one side at a time when balance and the knee permit.
4. Increase repetitions within the listed range.
5. Add an isometric hold after the final repetition.
6. Choose a harder angle, such as moving from wall push-ups to bench push-ups and then floor push-ups.
7. Stop most sets with approximately **2 good repetitions still possible**.

Do not use unstable doors, folding chairs, glass tables or lightweight furniture for rows, support or elevated exercises.

# 3. SESSION RULES

## Strength-day effort

- Most sets: **RPE 7–8**
- Finish with approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve**
- Do not train every set to failure
- Muscle fatigue is acceptable
- Joint pain, warmth, swelling or instability is not acceptable

## Rest periods

- Major upper-body supersets: **60–90 seconds**
- Demanding lower-body supersets: **90–120 seconds**
- Isolation, core, balance and rehabilitation exercises: **30–60 seconds**
- Longer rest is allowed on Saturday and Sunday to preserve form

## Progression

When an exercise is written as **3 × 8–12**:

1. Select a load that permits at least 8 clean repetitions.
2. Gradually add repetitions.
3. When all three sets reach 12 with approximately two repetitions remaining, increase load by the smallest available amount.
4. Return to approximately 8–9 repetitions.
5. For body-weight exercises, progress the angle, tempo, pause or unilateral difficulty before adding many extra sets.

## First two weeks

- Use **2 working sets for most exercises**
- Retain 3 sets only for the first main pair
- Use approximately 3 repetitions in reserve
- Record the knee response during training and the following morning

## Weeks 3–6

- Use the listed sets
- Finish most sets with approximately 2 repetitions in reserve
- Increase repetitions before weight

## Deload

Use a lighter week during **week 7**, or sooner when fatigue, poor sleep, joint irritation or declining performance accumulates:

- Reduce working sets by approximately 30–50%
- Use lighter loads
- Retain gentle mobility and symptom-free rehabilitation
- Do not chase personal records

# 4. KNEE TRAFFIC-LIGHT SYSTEM

## Green Day

- Pain 0–2/10
- No increased warmth or swelling
- No instability
- Knee is at normal baseline the following morning

**Action:** Follow the planned exercise and progress gradually.

## Yellow Day

- Pain 3–4/10
- Mild stiffness, tenderness or thickening
- Symptoms settle within 24 hours
- Movement confidence is reduced

**Action:** Reduce load, depth, repetitions or sets by 25–50%. Use the listed Yellow-Day alternative and do not progress that week.

## Red Day

- Increasing warmth or visible swelling
- Giving-way, locking or limping
- Pain 5/10 or higher
- Symptoms persist beyond 24–48 hours or worsen

**Action:** Stop loaded knee-bending work. Use only comfortable isometrics or gentle range of motion and arrange clinical assessment.

## Next-morning rule

A lower-body exercise is considered tolerated only when the knee returns to its usual baseline by the next morning.

## D. Bulgarian split-squat rules

### Green Day

Perform the exercise when:

- Starting pain is 0–2/10
- There is no warmth, thickening or swelling
- There is no current instability
- The knee was at baseline that morning

Technique:

- Use one-hand support
- Keep the rear-foot support low
- Begin with body weight or light resistance
- Use a comfortable depth
- Lower for approximately 3 seconds
- Track the front knee toward the second or third toe
- Stop with approximately 3 repetitions remaining

### Yellow Day

Use the first smooth and pain-free option:

1. Supported static split squat with rear foot on floor — 2 × 6–8/side
2. Short-Arc Quad Extension — 2 × 15–20/side
3. Terminal Knee Extension — 2 × 15–20/side
4. Shallow high-chair sit-to-stand — 2 × 6–8

### Red Day

Do not perform loaded knee bending. Use:

- Quad Set — 2–3 × 10
- Straight-Leg Raise — 2 × 10–12 if comfortable
- Short-Arc Quad Extension — 1–2 × 12–15 if comfortable

# 13. CONDITIONING TARGET

The strength sessions and supersets improve work capacity but do not fully replace aerobic activity.

## Current knee-sensitive phase

- Use flat walking, swimming or seated upper-body conditioning.
- Complete the Friday 10–15-minute block when the knee is calm.
- Complete Sunday’s 10–20-minute block when tolerated.
- Add short easy walks during the day.
- Avoid incline walking and cycling while they repeatedly provoke symptoms.

## Future progression

Once the knee is consistently stable:

1. Add walking duration before adding speed.
2. Reintroduce cycling only at very low resistance for approximately 5 minutes.
3. Use a seat height that avoids deep knee flexion.
4. Add 1–2 minutes before increasing resistance.
5. Stop if warmth, swelling or next-day symptoms appear.
6. Progress gradually toward approximately 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week.

## Food-building framework

### Protein

Eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, controlled portions of paneer, tofu, soy, dal, beans, lean meat or whey protein when needed.

### Carbohydrates

Rice, oats, potatoes, whole-grain bread or chapati, legumes and fruit. Adjust portions rather than eliminating carbohydrates.

### Healthy fats

Measured portions of nuts, seeds, olive oil, avocado and egg yolks. Oils, fried foods and nuts can raise calories rapidly.

### Quality rule

- Aim for 80–90% minimally processed food.
- Use the remaining 10–20% for planned flexibility.
- Avoid casually drinking calories.
- Treat sugary milkshakes as desserts.
- A protein shake counts toward calories.
- Avoid crash dieting.

## Calorie adjustment

- If average weight and waist do not decrease for **3 consecutive weeks**, reduce intake by approximately 100–150 kcal.
- If weight falls faster than approximately 0.5 kg per week, strength declines, recovery worsens or hair shedding increases, add approximately 100–150 kcal.
- Do not reduce protein when adjusting calories.

# 15. SLEEP AND RECOVERY

## Sleep target

At age 37, aim for:

> **7.5–8.5 hours of sleep per night**

- **7 hours** should be treated as the routine minimum, not the ideal target.
- During demanding training weeks, poor recovery or a calorie deficit, aim closer to **8–9 hours** when realistically possible.
- Keep wake time reasonably consistent.
- Reduce bright-screen use, heavy meals and intense work immediately before sleep.
- A short nap may help when nighttime sleep was limited, but it should not routinely replace adequate nighttime sleep.
- Persistent loud snoring, gasping, morning headaches or excessive daytime sleepiness should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## Other recovery practices

- Break up long periods of sitting with brief movement.
- Use the Monday and Friday sessions as genuinely lighter days.
- Do not convert active recovery into hidden high-intensity workouts.
- Keep at least one to two repetitions in reserve when sleep is poor.
- Reduce load or sets when knee symptoms or systemic fatigue increase.
- Use sunscreen on exposed skin and maintain hydration in Arizona heat.

# 16. HAIR AND DENTAL SUPPORT

## Hair

- Avoid crash dieting and severe calorie restriction.
- Maintain protein intake.
- Include dietary sources of iron, zinc, vitamin B12, vitamin D and omega-3 fats.
- Seek testing rather than beginning high-dose supplements blindly if shedding increases.
- Maintain consistent sleep and stress management.

## Teeth

- Brush for two minutes twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between teeth once daily.
- Reduce the frequency of sugary and acidic drinks.
- Rinse with water after coffee, shakes or acidic foods.
- Avoid sipping sugary drinks over several hours.
- Follow the dentist’s recommended check-up schedule.

# FINAL DIRECTION

Follow the first two weeks conservatively and learn the body-weight alternatives before they are needed. The goal is not to fill every available minute or perform every exercise with maximum weight. The goal is repeatable training, complete muscular coverage, progressive overload, controlled nutrition, adequate sleep and a knee that remains at baseline.

A 70–90-minute weekend allowance provides room for proper warm-up, complete lower-leg and core work, normal rest periods, conditioning and cooldown. It does not require 90 minutes every Saturday and Sunday. End the session once the planned work is complete or movement quality begins to decline.
