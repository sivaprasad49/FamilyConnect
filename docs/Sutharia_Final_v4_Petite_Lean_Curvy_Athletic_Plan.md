# SUTHARIA - FINAL v4 PETITE LEAN-CURVY ATHLETIC TRAINING & NUTRITION PLAN

**Profile:** 32F | 150 cm | approximately 66 kg

**Past history:** C-section in 2018 and VBAC in 2022; postpartum recovery is complete.

**Current considerations:** Intermittent joint inflammation; no current knee problem; no current wrist pain; no pull-ups or hanging exercises required.

**Primary goal:** Build a petite, lean-curvy athletic shape with rounded glutes, firm proportional legs, a tighter waist, a strong defined core, lightly toned arms and shoulders, an elegant upper back and posture, healthy joints, good energy, hair and teeth - without prioritizing maximum muscle size.

**Time:** One combined 50-60 minute morning or evening session including yoga/mobility, strength training, core, breathing and cooldown.

**Equipment:** Treadmill | stationary bike | bench | dumbbells | functional trainer/cables.

> This plan treats her as a fully recovered adult trainee. The delivery history remains relevant background, but it does not impose permanent postpartum restrictions. Pelvic-floor work is maintenance, not the center of the program.

# 1. GOALS AND SUCCESS MARKERS

## Main outcomes
- Reduce body fat gradually while preserving muscle, energy and hair health.
- Build a petite lean-curvy athletic shape rather than maximum size.
- Emphasize glutes, proportional legs, a firmer waist and elegant posture.
- Train every major muscle group at least twice per week.
- Build normal, progressive core strength without treating her as postpartum rehabilitation.
- Improve cardiovascular fitness, mobility and daily energy.
- Keep training sustainable within one 50-60 minute session.

## Shape priorities
1. Glute max and glute medius - rounded, lifted hip shape and pelvic stability
2. Quads and hamstrings - firm, balanced legs
3. Core and waist - strong rectus abdominis, deep core, side stability and anti-rotation
4. Upper back, lats and rear shoulders - posture and a smooth athletic back line
5. Moderate shoulders and arms - light definition without bodybuilding specialization
6. Calves, tibialis, feet, grip and neck - complete proportions and movement quality

## How progress will be judged
- Seven-day average body weight, not one isolated weigh-in
- Waist and hip measurements every two weeks
- Front, side and back photos every four weeks
- Strength and repetition progress on the main exercises
- Joint comfort during training and the following morning
- Energy, sleep, menstrual symptoms and workout consistency

## Modify when needed
- Reduce deep Utkatasana, long lunges or repeated low-plank transitions during a joint flare.

- Past childbirth alone is not a reason to avoid normal core work; present symptoms and exercise control are the deciding factors.

# 7. PELVIC-FLOOR MAINTENANCE

Her postpartum recovery is complete, so the pelvic floor is trained like any other supporting system: enough for function, not as daily rehabilitation.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| Monday | Six gentle endurance holds with full relaxation |
| Wednesday | Reverse Kegels and diaphragmatic relaxation |
| Sunday | Gentle breath-synchronised contractions |
| Other strength days | Normal exhale during effort; no continuous squeezing |

- Do not repeatedly stop urine flow as exercise.
- Do not force maximal contractions or squeeze the buttocks and thighs.
- Reduce or stop pelvic-floor contractions if they cause urgency, pelvic pain, difficulty emptying the bladder/bowel or discomfort during intercourse.
- Leakage, heaviness, pelvic pressure or pain should be assessed rather than hidden by doing more Kegels.

# 8. JOINT-INFLAMMATION TRAFFIC-LIGHT SYSTEM

## Green day
- Pain 0-2/10
- No increased warmth or swelling
- Normal movement and stability
- Back to baseline the next morning
**Action:** Complete the session and progress gradually.

## Yellow day
- Pain 3-4/10
- Mild stiffness or inflammation
- Symptoms settle within 24 hours
**Action:** Reduce weight, range or sets by 25-50%; replace the provoking movement; keep cardio easy.

## Red day
- Visible swelling, redness or increased warmth
- Loss of function or severe pain
- Pain 5/10 or higher
- Symptoms persist beyond 24-48 hours or worsen
**Action:** Stop loading the affected joint. Use comfortable movement or rest rather than automatically choosing one machine. Seek medical advice for significant or recurrent symptoms.

# 9. MENSTRUAL READINESS GUIDANCE

Training is adjusted from actual symptoms rather than fixed calendar-day rules.

## Train normally when
- Energy and sleep are reasonable
- Bleeding and cramps are manageable
- Joints feel stable
- Warm-up improves movement

## Reduce training by 20-40% when
- Cramps, heavy bleeding, migraine or fatigue are significant
- Joint inflammation increases
- Sleep is poor
- Warm-up does not improve discomfort

- Drop one set from each exercise
- Use RPE 5-6 instead of 7-8
- Replace hard training with yoga and walking
- Skip forceful breathing practices when uncomfortable

# 10. INTENSITY, REST AND PROGRESSION

## RPE and repetitions in reserve
- Most working sets: RPE 7-8, meaning approximately 2-3 good repetitions remain possible.
- Joint pain is never treated as productive effort.
- Training to failure is unnecessary for this goal.

## Rest
- 60-90 seconds after each completed superset.
- Up to 2 minutes after demanding lower-body rounds.
- 30-45 seconds for small-muscle or isolation work.

## Weeks 1-2 - technique and baseline
- Use 2 working sets for most exercises; keep 3 sets for the first main pair.
- Finish with approximately 3 repetitions still possible.
- Record joint response during the workout and the next morning.

## Weeks 3-8 - progressive training
- Use the listed sets.
- Finish most sets with approximately 2 repetitions still possible.
- Increase repetitions before increasing weight.

## Double progression
1. Reach the top of the repetition range for every set with clean form.
2. Keep approximately two repetitions in reserve.
3. Confirm there is no adverse joint response by the next morning.
4. Then add the smallest available weight increment and return to the lower end of the repetition range.

## Deload
- Every 7th or 8th week, or earlier if fatigue accumulates, reduce working sets by 30-50%.
- Keep comfortable yoga, easy cardio and mobility.
- Do not chase personal records during a deload.

# 15. FOOD QUALITY, HAIR, TEETH, SLEEP AND APPEARANCE

## Food quality
- Use an 80/20 approach: mostly minimally processed food with planned flexibility.
- Prioritize eggs, dairy/Greek yogurt, chicken, tofu, paneer, soy, dals, beans and whey when needed for protein.
- Use rice, chapati, oats, potatoes, fruit and whole grains as measured carbohydrate sources.
- Include vegetables, legumes, nuts/seeds and calcium-rich foods regularly.
- Plan sweets and fried snacks instead of grazing unpredictably.

## Hair
- Avoid crash dieting and rapid weight loss.
- Maintain protein consistently.
- Include iron, zinc, B12, vitamin D and omega-3 food sources.
- Seek testing rather than blindly using high-dose supplements if shedding increases.

## Teeth
- Brush for 2 minutes twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between teeth once daily.
- Reduce the frequency of sugary and acidic drinks/snacks.
- Rinse with water after coffee, shakes or acidic foods.

## Recovery and appearance
- Aim for 7.5-8.5 hours of sleep.
- Maintain hydration and use sunscreen on exposed skin.
- Use posture exercises consistently: rows, face pulls, rear delts, wall slides and carries.
- Choose clothing fit, grooming and hairstyle that complement her proportions; appearance is more than body-fat percentage.

# FINAL DIRECTION

Follow the plan for eight weeks before making major changes. The first goal is consistent technique and joint tolerance. The body-shape result will come from months of progressive strength training, controlled nutrition, adequate protein, cardio fitness and recovery - not from extreme dieting or excessive daily exercise.

The desired outcome is strong, feminine and athletic: rounded glutes, balanced legs, a firmer waist, a defined back and arms, good posture and high energy - without chasing maximum muscle size.
